# WASM Game of Life

Around 2021 and 2022, I was trying to understand where Rust could fit into the Web. The old [Rust and WebAssembly book](https://rustwasm.github.io/docs/book/) was my map. It is a legacy guide now, but it still explains the important shape of the problem very well: let Rust do the computation, let JavaScript own the browser, and use WebAssembly as the boundary.

The [Game of Life project](https://github.com/GarfieldZHU/Aloha.zone.io/tree/master/wasm/rust-wasm/wasm-game-of-life) was the part that made the idea concrete for me. Rust keeps the universe and advances each generation. `wasm-bindgen` exposes the Rust API, `wasm-pack` builds the package, and a small JavaScript loop draws the cells on a canvas. The interesting part is not the rules of Conway's game. It is watching a Rust data structure become something I can click and play with in a browser.

## The playground

This is the little build I kept coming back to. Click cells to make a pattern, pause it, and reset the world when it becomes too chaotic. The frame counter is a nice reminder that this is still a browser program: the WebAssembly module does the state update, while JavaScript schedules and paints the frames.
